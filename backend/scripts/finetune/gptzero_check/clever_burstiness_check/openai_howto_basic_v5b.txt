To brew pour-over coffee at home, it is necessary to have a pour-over dripper, a paper filter, freshly ground coffee, a kettle, and hot water. The first step is to place the paper filter inside the dripper and rinse it with hot water. This rinsing removes any paper taste and warms both the dripper and the cup or carafe. The rinse water should be discarded before adding the coffee grounds.

The next step is to grind coffee beans to a medium-coarse consistency, resembling sea salt. For one cup of coffee, approximately 15 to 18 grams of coffee are required. Then, the dripper should be positioned on top of the cup or carafe, and the coffee grounds added to the filter, leveling them gently. Water should be heated to just below boiling, about 200 degrees Fahrenheit (93 degrees Celsius).

The brewing process begins by pouring a small amount of water evenly over the coffee grounds, sufficient to saturate them. After this, one should wait for about 30 seconds. This phase, known as blooming, allows gases to escape and enhances flavor extraction. Then, the remaining water must be poured slowly and steadily in a circular motion to cover all the grounds evenly. It is important to pour in increments and pause as needed, aiming for a total brew time of approximately three to four minutes.

Once the dripping slows and the water has passed entirely through the coffee grounds, the dripper should be removed. The fresh pour-over coffee is then ready to be enjoyed. With continued practice, one can adjust the grind size, coffee quantity, and pouring speed to achieve the preferred flavor and strength.
