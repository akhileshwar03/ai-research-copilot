The blue color of the sky results from the interaction between sunlight and the Earth's atmosphere. Sunlight appears white but consists of a combination of all the colors in the visible spectrum. Each color corresponds to a specific wavelength, with red and orange having longer wavelengths and blue and violet having shorter wavelengths.

When sunlight passes through the atmosphere, it encounters a mixture of gas molecules, primarily nitrogen and oxygen. The longer wavelengths, such as red and yellow, travel through the atmosphere with minimal scattering. This is why direct sunlight retains a warm, yellowish appearance. In contrast, the shorter blue wavelengths are scattered in many directions by the air molecules. This process is called Rayleigh scattering. Because blue light is scattered more than other colors, it spreads throughout the sky, creating the blue appearance overhead.

Violet light has an even shorter wavelength than blue light, but the sky does not appear violet for two reasons. Firstly, the sun emits more blue light than violet light. Secondly, the human eye is more sensitive to blue light than violet light. As a result, the scattered light that reaches the observer is predominantly blue. Thus, when one looks at the sky on a clear day, one is observing scattered blue light caused by its interaction with atmospheric gases.
