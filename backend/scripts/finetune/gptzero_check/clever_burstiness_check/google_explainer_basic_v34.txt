On a clear afternoon, the sky appears blue rather than green, red, or transparent due to how sunlight interacts with the atmosphere. Although sunlight seems white, it actually consists of all the colors found in a rainbow. Each color corresponds to a particular wavelength, with red and orange having long, gentle waves and blue and violet having short, rapid ones.

As sunlight passes through Earth's atmosphere, it encounters molecules of gases like nitrogen and oxygen. The longer wavelengths of red and yellow light mostly travel straight through these molecules, which is why sunlight often looks warm and yellow when it reaches the ground. In contrast, the shorter blue light waves collide with the gas molecules and scatter in all directions. This scattering effect is called Rayleigh scattering. Because blue light is scattered so widely overhead, it gives the sky its characteristic blue glow.

You might ask why the sky is not violet, since violet light has an even shorter wavelength than blue. The reason is that the sun emits much more blue light than violet light, and human eyes are more sensitive to blue. Therefore, when you look up on a sunny day, you are seeing blue light scattered by the atmosphere, filling the sky like a vast, glowing canopy.
