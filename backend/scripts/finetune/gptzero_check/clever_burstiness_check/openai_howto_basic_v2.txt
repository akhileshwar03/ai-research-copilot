So, you want to make your own pour-over coffee at home. It’s not rocket science, but there are some steps you can't skip if you want anything close to a decent cup. First, grab a pour-over dripper, a paper filter, fresh coffee grounds, a kettle, and hot water. Yeah, basic stuff, but don't underestimate the filter rinse—that’s key. Pop the paper filter into the dripper, pour hot water over it to get rid of that paper taste and warm up your gear. Dump that rinse water because you don’t want it messing with your brew.

Now, grind your beans. Not too fine, not too coarse—think sea salt texture. Measure out 15 to 18 grams for one cup, no shortcuts. Put the dripper on your mug or carafe, add the grounds, and smooth them out a bit so water spreads evenly. Heat your water to just under boiling—around 200°F or 93°C (yeah, the nerdy detail matters).

Here’s where it gets interesting: pour a little water over the grounds, just enough to wet them all, then wait 30 seconds. That’s the bloom—coffee releases gases and flavors get ready to come out. Then, pour the rest of the water slowly and steadily in circles so every bit of coffee gets attention. Don’t rush this part. Pour in stages, take short breaks, aim for your total brew time to be about three or four minutes.

When the water stops dripping, yank off the dripper and dig in. Your coffee’s done. What’s cool is, once you nail this routine, you can tweak everything—grind size, coffee weight, how you pour—to get your perfect cup. Have you tried messing with any of those variables yet?
