Brewing pour-over coffee at home provides a simple way to enjoy a fresh cup while allowing precise control over its flavor and strength. The essential equipment consists of a pour-over dripper, a paper filter, freshly ground coffee, a kettle, and hot water. Begin by placing the paper filter inside the dripper and rinsing it with hot water. This step removes any paper taste and preheats both the dripper and the cup or carafe positioned below. Once the rinse water is discarded, the coffee grounds are added.

Coffee beans must be ground to a medium-coarse texture. This should resemble the size of sea salt grains. About 15 to 18 grams of coffee works well for a single cup. Place the dripper on top of the cup or carafe. Then add the grounds to the filter, spreading them evenly. Heat the water to just under boiling—around 200 degrees Fahrenheit (93 degrees Celsius).

Begin by pouring a small quantity of water evenly over the grounds, just enough to saturate them, and allow approximately 30 seconds for the bloom phase. This step permits gases to escape, enhancing flavor extraction. Following the bloom, continue pouring the remaining water slowly and steadily in a circular pattern to cover all the grounds. It is advisable to pour in stages, pausing as necessary, so that the total brewing time falls between three and four minutes.

When the dripping slows and the water has fully passed through the coffee, remove the dripper. With continued practice, adjustments to grind size, coffee quantity, and pouring speed can refine the brew to personal preference.
