Making pour-over coffee at home is pretty straightforward and lets you control how strong and flavorful your cup turns out. First, you’ll need a pour-over dripper, a paper filter, fresh coffee grounds, a kettle, and hot water. Begin by placing the paper filter into the dripper, then rinse it thoroughly with hot water to get rid of any paper taste and warm up both the dripper and your mug or carafe. After that, make sure to pour out the rinse water before adding the coffee grounds.

Grind your beans to a medium-coarse texture, similar to sea salt. You'll want about 15 to 18 grams of coffee for a single cup. Then, set the dripper on your cup or carafe and add the coffee grounds, leveling them off gently. Heat the water to just under boiling, around 200°F (93°C), so it’s hot enough to extract the flavors without burning the coffee.

Pour a little water evenly over the grounds to wet them completely, then wait about 30 seconds. This bloom lets gases escape and improves the taste. After that, slowly pour the rest of the water in circles to cover all the grounds evenly, making sure you pour in stages if you need to so the whole brewing takes about three to four minutes.

When the dripping slows and the water’s gone through, take off the dripper and enjoy your fresh coffee. As you try this more, you can tweak the grind size, how much coffee you use, and your pour rate to get the exact cup you like.
