So, if you want to make pour-over coffee at home—it's not rocket science, but you do need a few things: a dripper, paper filters, freshly ground beans, a kettle, and hot water. First, shove a paper filter into the dripper and rinse it with hot water. Yeah, you’re literally washing paper—sounds odd, but this gets rid of that weird paper taste and warms up your dripper and cup. Dump that rinse water before you add any coffee.

Then, grind your beans to a texture like coarse sea salt (don’t just eyeball it), and grab about 15 to 18 grams for a single cup. Put the dripper on your cup or carafe, dump the grounds in, and even them out a bit. Heat water until it’s just shy of boiling—around 200°F or 93°C if you wanna be exact.

Now, here’s where it gets a little tricky: pour a small amount of water over the grounds, just enough to soak them, and hold up for 30 seconds. This “bloom” lets the coffee release gases—think of it as giving your coffee a chance to breathe. After that, pour the rest slowly, in circles, making sure every bit of grounds gets wet. Don’t rush; pour, pause, pour, pause. The whole brew should take about three to four minutes.

Eventually, the dripping slows to a stop. Take off the dripper, and bam—you’ve got yourself a fresh pour-over cup. With a bit of trial and error adjusting grind size, coffee amount, and how fast you pour, you’ll find your sweet spot. Want to mess around with the strength? Go for it. It’s your brew.
