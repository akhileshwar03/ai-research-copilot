You want fresh coffee that doesn’t taste like burnt rubber or mud sludge, right? So, grab a pour-over dripper—yeah, the cone-shaped thing—a paper filter, and some freshly ground beans. Oh, and don’t forget a kettle filled with hot water. Toss the paper filter into the dripper, then rinse it with hot water until it’s steaming. This gets rid of that annoying paper flavor and heats up everything so your cup doesn’t turn cold the second you pour.

Now, toss out that pre-ground stuff—grind your beans fresh. Aim for something like sea salt, not dust or chunks. For a single cup, measure out between 15 and 18 grams. Put the dripper on your cup or carafe, then dump the grounds in and flatten them a bit—don’t pack it like you’re making a burger patty though.

Heat your water close to boiling but not boiling exactly—about 200°F or 93°C. Start by pouring a little water over the grounds—just enough to wet them all—then wait for about 30 seconds. You’ll see the coffee puff up a bit; that’s the bloom letting out gases. Don’t rush this part.

After that, pour the rest slowly and in circles, making sure every bit of the grounds gets some water love. Don’t pour it all at once; take breaks. The whole thing should take between three to four minutes, tops.

When the dripping slows to a crawl and the water’s mostly through, yank off the dripper. Your cup is ready. With a little trial and error, you can mess with the grind size, how much coffee you use, or how fast you pour to find the sweet spot that makes your perfect cup. Easy enough, right?
