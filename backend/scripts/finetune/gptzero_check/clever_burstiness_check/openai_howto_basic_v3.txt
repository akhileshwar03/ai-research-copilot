If you want to make pour-over coffee at home, it’s way simpler than you might think, and you get to tweak the taste exactly how you like it. First, grab your pour-over dripper and slip a paper filter inside. Then, give the filter a quick rinse with hot water—this gets rid of that weird paper flavor and warms up the dripper and your mug or carafe. Dump out the rinse water before adding coffee.

Now grind your beans to a texture that’s kind of like sea salt—not too fine, not too coarse. Use about 15 to 18 grams for a single cup. Set the dripper on your cup or carafe and pour the grounds into the filter, smoothing them out gently. Heat water until just under boiling, around 200°F (93°C). Got that? Good.

Here’s the trick: pour just enough water to wet all the grounds and then stop. Wait 30 seconds. This is when the coffee puffs up—called the bloom—and it lets stuff escape so your coffee tastes better. After that, pour the rest of the water slowly in circles over the grounds, covering everything evenly. Don’t rush—spread the pouring out over three to four minutes.

When the dripping slows down, yank off the dripper and you’re set. This method takes a little trial and error, messing with grind size, how much coffee you use, and how fast you pour to get that perfect cup. Sounds like a hassle? Maybe.
