Okay, so this Apex Pro Mid-Range Mechanical Keyboard—they’ve managed to cram in way more than you’d expect for the price. You start typing, and those brown-tactile switches don’t just click like a jackhammer; instead, they give you a soft bump that tells you, “Hey, you actually pressed the key,” without the noisy bang. It’s less annoying for hours of typing but still sharp enough if you’re gaming and need your presses quick and sure. That’s kind of rare in this price bracket, right?

Now, the keycaps—double-shot PBT—don’t just look decent; they actually keep their feel, no shiny slickness even after you’ve hammered away for weeks. Plus, the slight curve on each key? It’s subtle but your fingers sort of find their way without thinking about it, which is nice. The whole thing’s built with an aluminum frame that’s not just for show—it’s brushed, sturdy, and cuts down vibrations so the keyboard feels solid, not like a cheap toy. And they didn’t cheap out on the cable either—a USB-C one that you can detach (finally). You know how often smaller brands skimp on that?

The stabilizers on the big keys? Well-oiled out of the box, so you won’t hear that annoying rattle that often ruins the vibe on less expensive boards. That little touch? Usually ignored but makes a big difference. 

Oh, and before I forget, it’s packed with extras—programmable macro keys, per-key RGB lighting that’s not just for show, and software that lets you tweak stuff without turning into a full-time programmer. The price lands around $100 to $120, which is kind of a steal considering these are features you usually only see way up in the $150-plus territory.

So yeah, this one isn’t just another mid-range keyboard; it actually nails typing comfort, build quality, and customization options without bleeding your wallet dry. Would I recommend it to pros or casual users? Totally. It’s a solid all-arounder.
