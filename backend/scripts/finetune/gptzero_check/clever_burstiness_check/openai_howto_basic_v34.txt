Brewing pour-over coffee at home offers a straightforward way to enjoy a fresh cup while maintaining control over its flavor and strength. To begin, gather a pour-over dripper, a paper filter, freshly ground coffee, a kettle, and hot water. Place the paper filter into the dripper and rinse it with hot water. This removes any paper taste and warms both the dripper and the cup or carafe beneath. Be sure to discard the rinse water before adding the coffee.

Grind the coffee beans to a medium-coarse texture, similar to sea salt. Use about 15 to 18 grams of coffee for a single cup. Set the dripper on your cup or carafe, then add the grounds to the filter, leveling them gently. Heat water to just below boiling, approximately 200 degrees Fahrenheit (93 degrees Celsius).

Begin by pouring a small amount of water evenly over the grounds to saturate them, then wait about 30 seconds. This blooming stage allows gases to escape, improving the coffee’s flavor extraction. After the bloom, pour the remaining water slowly and steadily in a circular motion, ensuring all the grounds are covered. Pour in stages, pausing as necessary, so the total brewing time is around three to four minutes.

When the dripping slows and the water has passed through the coffee, remove the dripper. Your fresh pour-over coffee is ready to enjoy. With experience, adjusting the grind size, coffee amount, and pour speed lets you customize your perfect cup.
