Making pour-over coffee at home is straightforward and lets you control how strong and flavorful your cup turns out. You’ll need a pour-over dripper, a paper filter, freshly ground coffee, a kettle, and hot water. Start by putting the paper filter into the dripper and rinsing it with hot water. This washes away any paper taste and warms up the dripper along with your cup or carafe. Don’t forget to throw out the rinse water before you add the coffee.

Next, grind your coffee beans to a medium-coarse texture, something like sea salt. For a single cup, use about 15 to 18 grams of ground coffee. Set the dripper over your cup or carafe and pour the grounds into the filter, spreading them out evenly but gently. Heat your water to just under boiling—about 200 degrees Fahrenheit or 93 degrees Celsius.

Pour a small amount of water evenly over the grounds to wet them completely, then wait for around 30 seconds. This “bloom” lets gases escape, which improves the flavor. After that, slowly pour the rest of the water in a steady, circular motion so all the grounds get wet. Pour in stages if you need to, aiming for a total brew time of three to four minutes.

When the dripping slows and the water has fully passed through the coffee, take off the dripper and enjoy your fresh cup. As you get more comfortable, you can play with the grind size, amount of coffee, and pouring speed until you find exactly how you like it.
