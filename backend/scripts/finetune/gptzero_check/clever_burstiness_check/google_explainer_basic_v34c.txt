Have you ever looked up on a clear afternoon and wondered why the sky’s blue instead of green, red, or totally clear? It all comes down to how sunlight interacts with the air around us.

Sunlight looks white, but it's actually a mix of all the colors in a rainbow. Each color moves in waves. These waves aren't all the same length, though. Red and orange have long, slow waves, but blue and violet have waves that are shorter and faster.

When sunlight hits the atmosphere, it runs into a thick mix of gas molecules—mostly nitrogen and oxygen. The long waves of red and yellow pass through more easily, so sunlight looks warm and yellow when it reaches you directly. Blue waves, though, are shorter and bump into those air molecules a lot, so they get scattered in all directions. This scattering is called Rayleigh scattering, and it’s what causes the sky to glow with blue light everywhere you look.

You might think the sky should be violet because violet waves are shorter than blue ones. But the sun actually sends out more blue light than violet, and our eyes pick up blue better too. So when you’re outside on a sunny day, you’re really watching blue light bounce off the air and hit your eyes, almost like a cosmic pinball game.
