Pour over is one of my favorite methods for making delicious home brews because it focuses on the unique flavors in whatever beans I'm using that day. Here's a quick guide showing beginners how they can make great pour overs at home.

What you'll need:

Fresh coffee beans Burr grinder Pour-over brewer (Hario V60 or Kalita Wave) Paper filter Gooseneck kettle Scale and/or timer Mug/Carafe Hot water

Step 1: Measure out your dose. Depending on the strength of this coffee, start with a ratio between a tablespoon of coffee per ounce all the way to up to one-and-a-half tablespoons for each cup — I usually just eyeball it. You can always play around if you'd like but starting here will give you something good.

Step 2: Grind your beans! Don't have an electric grinder yet? Click this link for my favorite choice of manual or electric grinders and then come back to continue reading (just kidding!). Grind up those beans and add them into the filter basket, being careful not go too deep as you want some coffee resting on top. For good taste it's imperative that your freshly ground your own grounds.

Step 3: Add some water! Preheat your brewer by pouring hot water over each of the walls; this eliminates any paper or plastic taste and keeps everything warm from a temperature standpoint. The filter should be saturated at this point as well, discard water once complete!

Step 4: Bloom time! Give yourself about thirty to forty-five seconds here so it allows the coffee grounds some space for them to release gases that are trapped in their little seed bodies.

Bloom by pouring enough hot water on top of your grounds to just saturate (it will look like a wet sponge). Start timer before pouring and wait until time's up! This method adds lots of flavor as long as you use good-quality water. If it makes sense, I also do my bloom in stages instead of adding one whole cup all at once so the bottom layer has plenty of time to steep while there is still head room available.

Step 5: Pour away Slowly add your next rounds or shots pouring just enough on top for saturation and slowly circle around until you've poured a round that equals about twenty-five-to-thirty ounces! You don't have to go crazy and pour it all in at once so do it slow, make little circles — it's what creates those flavors.

Step 6: Let 'er hang, baby! Pour the water down evenly and allow it to drip through until no more water drips on top of grounds. The time is approximately between two-and-a-half-three-and-a-half minutes depending on how big or small your pour was in total and how many grams the cup weighed.

Step 7: Sip that bad boy! Once you've removed the brewer give your coffee a little swirl (or two, three), enjoy!
