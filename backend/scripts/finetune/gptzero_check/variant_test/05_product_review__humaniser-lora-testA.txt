The XYZ Mechanical Keyboard strikes that sweet spot of performance and price so many keyboards in its mid-range bracket fail to hit

If you've been reading our articles on gaming laptops or desktops recently then chances are we'll have mentioned to you before how important a good quality mechanical keyboard is for gamers, programmers and writers. And it's not just us who think that either; even the most casual of users would agree with me if I said they'd prefer an accurate clicky key under their finger over a mushier membrane one any day.

So we've been on the lookout to find out which mechanical keyboard you can get your hands, fingers and eyes onto for as little cash. And now it's time that I introduce my latest favourite: The XYZ Mechanical Keyboard

The first thing anyone will notice about this keyboard is how nice typing feels - a very satisfying clicky-ness in fact from the tactile switches that provide just enough resistance to prevent accidental presses without being too heavy or stiff, and yet still allow for smooth keystrokes over long periods of time. Whether you're writing an epic fantasy novel while sipping on your morning coffee; programming away at work before heading home with a gaming rig set up in front of the TV - this keyboard has got you covered.

The build quality is also pretty impressive too, though not quite as robust or expensive-feeling than some high-end aluminium models. It's still made from plastic but it does have that nice matte finish to stop fingerprints and minor scratches whilst maintaining a solid weight without being overbearing with the heft either - in other words: perfect for daily use.

The keycaps are also of decent quality, using PBT (Polybutylene terephthalate) material which is preferred by keyboard enthusiasts as it resists shine better than ABS plastic and doesn't wear down so quickly. The legends on each individual cap remain bright even after hours upon hours spent typing away at a furious pace.

The detachable USB-C cable also helps with portability, though this does mean the included charging adapter (which is pretty small) might not fit in all laptop chargers - but that's easily remedied by purchasing an external one. The keycaps are easy to replace too if you happen upon any particularly troublesome ones after a few months of use.

As far as value for money goes, this keyboard hits the sweet spot between features and price point pretty well with its customizable RGB lighting (not quite on par quality wise but still looks good), programmable keys that can be set up via software in advance to suit your workflow needs - all while being more than capable of supporting gaming applications. It doesn't have some premium extras like hot-swappable switches, wireless connectivity or fancy design features which are common with higher-end models.

But for those who don't need such bells and whistles but want a reliable keyboard that performs well at an affordable price point then this is the one to get - I can personally vouch as much.
