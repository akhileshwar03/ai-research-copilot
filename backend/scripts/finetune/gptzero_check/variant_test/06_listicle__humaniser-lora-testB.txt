5 Small Habits That Quietly Improved My Sleep

It's always a constant challenge trying to get some good sleep with everything going on in life; the stress and lack of time often seem too much for your mind or body. Over time I've stumbled upon various ways that made getting my beauty rest happen more easily — not major lifestyle changes, but small things that could be implemented without completely overhauling anything.

These little tweaks may only have been a whisper in the back-end of day-to-day living and didn't require a total overhaul or change. Yet it still improved the quality of sleep at night as well as being able to fall asleep more easily:

Dimmed The Lights An Hour Before Bed

The light is super bright around here, but not all lights! I recently started dimming the main overhead lighting in my house about an hour before bed and also making sure that no screens are lit up for about 30-minutes or so as they emit a huge amount of blue-light which throws off your circadian rhythm.

Keep A Regular Sleep Schedule Even On The Weekends

For some reason people have this idea where it's okay to stay awake past their typical bedtime on the weekends because you can wake-up later. When I really started keeping my regular sleep schedule even during these days, both me and my body were so happy.

Drink Herbal Tea Instead of Caffeine Late In The Afternoon

Coffee is one thing that has become super hard for me to part ways with especially in the morning — but I also don't like using it as a way to energize myself later on throughout day either. Instead, replacing caffeine with herbal tea helped reduce stimulating effects when I really wanted something to help get my mind relaxed.

Practiced A 5-Minute Gratitude Reflection

I spend about five-minutes thinking of three different things during the course of your day that made you happy or grateful for before finally shutting off all lights and crawling into bed. It was a simple mental exercise, but helped take some anxiety out of winding down at night.

Keep The Bedroom Cool and Comfortable At 60-67°F (15-19°C)

Making sure the temperature inside my bedroom was set between those temperatures helps get me to sleep faster as well as keep it for longer. This may seem obvious, but sometimes we can forget that just getting your body into a more comfortable resting state will also do wonders.

You may not be able to fall asleep immediately after making these changes and in fact they won't all work on the same person or completely change things at once; however there is little need for overhauling big habits when small tweaks make an impact.
