I came back from some side-project that spent months on - only for the whole thing quietly die out.

Initially it was a personal failure but then thinking about this made me realize what really mattered wasn't getting it done or not as much how I went at doing things.<p>I had an idea to build something niche in productivity. Excited, i jumped straight into building that up and convinced myself if its good enough people would flock towards using my product. But the step of figuring out whether there is a problem worth solving for real potential users was ignored completely.

And when finally I launched it didn't really work - no one cared about another tool like mine.<p>That taught me early validation, customer discovery and all that jazz before you start building something don't ignore these steps. It's easy to fall in love with an idea but if its not validated by the people who will use your product then even great execution doesn't matter.

Failure is just a part of success - this project reminded me I have got some work ahead for myself and need more listening than talking, so that my passion can be put into purpose.
