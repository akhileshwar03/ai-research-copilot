You've seen how sometimes the sky turns an absolutely breathtaking shade of red or orange when the sun is setting in the horizon. Isn't it amazing? Have you ever wondered why?

Well, here's your answer: The Earth's atmosphere makes all that happen!

The Sun during daytime appears higher up and its light travels a shorter distance through the atmosphere to reach our eyes. You probably know by now that sunlight is made of several colors - red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo and violet which when mixed together appear white.

The Earth's atmosphere scatters most of these colors as the sun's light passes through it especially the shorter wavelengths (blue, violet) but they're scattered out in different directions. That's why we see a mostly blue sky during daytime.

At sunset however things change dramatically. The sun is lower on the horizon so its light has to travel a much thicker layer of atmosphere before reaching us. Because of this longer journey through our atmosphere most of the blue and violet wavelengths get scattered away from our line of sight leaving behind the reds, oranges and yellows which don't scatter as easily and continue straight ahead in our direction.

That's why we see the sky near the horizon glow with warm hues of red and orange. The particles like dust, pollutants or even water droplets in the air can further enhance this effect by scattering out these shorter wavelengths much more intensively making sunsets look even fancier!

So next time you see a burning sunset remember it's all thanks to the way sunlight dances through our atmosphere scattering colors in its wake painting the sky with nature's own palette. Quite beautiful, isn't it?
