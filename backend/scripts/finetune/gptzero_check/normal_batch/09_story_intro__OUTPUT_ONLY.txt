The kitchen smelled like cinnamon and old wood—two scents that hugged me close when perched on stool by Grandma's worn oak table. Morning light filtered through lace curtains, casting lacy patterns in the flour dusted countertops. Grandma's hands moved as if she were performing an old dance move, cracking eggs with such ease my fingers sometimes tangled around whisking batter. I watched wide-eyed and eager to imitate her every motion—though my hands fumbled and eggshells slipped into the bowl like slippery fish.

"Cooking is more than just following a recipe," said Grandma softly as she caught me scooping up broken shells with an overzealous attempt at folding in flour. "It's a way to tell stories, to share love without words."

Her voice was warm and steady—a lighthouse for the whirlwind of sizzling pans and bubbling pots. I remember how she'd hum old songs as she stirred and how her eyes would sparkle when our kitchen filled with the smell of caramelizing onions or the steam rising from a fresh-baked pie.

That summer, the kitchen became our haven—where time slowed to a stop for lessons that stretched beyond measuring cups and temperatures. Every dish carried some memory, a piece of Grandma's past woven into her present. Through burnt cookies and perfect pies I came to understand learning to cook with Grandma was about more than food—it was about heritage, patience, the quiet joy in creating something with your own hands.

And as the sun dipped below the horizon, casting golden hues over the cluttered countertops, I realized there were other things inheriting too.
